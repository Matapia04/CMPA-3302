# Shouryuken! About Fighting Games! 
## What are Fighting Games? 
Born in the Arcades scattered throughout Japan & America, fighting games have grown a passionate community rich in culture and competition. Fighting games are strictly 2 player games where each player controls 1 or a team of fighters in a competition of virtual fisticuffs. Fighting games come in a variety of art and game styles, but can broadly fit under 3 subgenres. 
## 2D Fighting Games
> 2D Fighting games aren't strictly referring to art style, rather the plane of movement in which they're allowed to move: Up, Down, Left, and Right. Yet within this sub-genre of Fighting game, there's enough innovation to differentiate them even further.

## Fighting Game Lingo
Terminology commonly used throughout all Fighting Games: 
* Low: A move that hits low, cannot be blocked while standing
* Overhead: A move that hits high, cannot be blocked while crouching
* Footsies: Used in tandem with "Neutral", refers to the specific movements of your character when performing attacks, and how it allows interaction for your opponent to do the same. If you press the "kick" button and miss, your opponent can hit your characters extended leg and perform a combo.
* Neutral: The flow of the game where you and your opponent try to land a blow
* DP: Originating from Street Fighter, refers to both the general application of a special move to knock your opponent away, as well as a specific series of motions to use the move. 
* OtG: "On the Ground", after hitting a character with a combo or special move, you're allowed to hit them one more time on the ground.
* Super: Catch-all name for the big ultimate attack your character uses in exchange for all(or most) of your resources
* Happy Birthday: Used in Tag Fighters, if your attacks hit 2 of your opponents characters at the same time
* DHC: Originating from Marvel vs Capcom, "Delayed Hyper Combo" refers to using a super, and quickly swapping into another character and using super again.
* EX: Refers to using your resources to buff up a special move
* Oki: Originating from the Japanese word "Okizime", or "Wake up Offense", refers to the situation in which your opponent is knocked down and getting back up, severely limiting how they can respond to any of your actions.
* HKD: Hard Knockdown, caused by specific attacks that cause your opponent to fall to the ground and unable to roll away, allowing you to apply Oki
* QCF: Abbreviation of the motion input "Quarter Circle Forward"
* QCB: Abbreviation of the motion input "Quarter Circle Back"
* Groove: Originating from Capcom vs SNK 2, it's a special set of rules/mechanics you can apply to your team of characters.
* Shoto: Originating from Street Fighter, any character with a Fireball move, a DP, and a Tatsu is commonly referred to as a "Shoto", and viewed as a beginner friendly character that's easy to understand and play.
* Tatsu: Originating from Street Fighter, a Tatsu is any move that's a spinning kick across the screen, passing through projectiles. 
* Incoming: Common in Tag Fighters, when forcing an enemy to tag out to a different character, or defeating one, if you are free to act while they transition to a new character, any move you do during this period is "incoming"
* X - Factor: Originating from Marvel Vs Capcom 3, "X-Factor" is a mechanic used to grant your characters a huge boost to their attack strength and ability to combo.
